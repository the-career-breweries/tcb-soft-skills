## PART B — Context, Assumptions & Anticipated Problems

| Field | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Learner Profile & Assumed Knowledge** | University students preparing for placements or group discussions. They have sufficient vocabulary to hold a conversation but often listen simply to reply, rather than listening to understand. They may lack the specific functional phrases used in corporate environments to acknowledge another speaker. |
| **Personal Aim (Trainer Development)** | I want to ensure my instructions for the role-play activity are crystal clear by using Instruction Checking Questions (ICQs) before they begin. I also want to minimize my own speaking time during the Free Practice stage. |
| **Anticipated Problems — Learners** | **Problem 1:** Learners might just repeat the partner's words verbatim instead of summarizing/paraphrasing.<br>**Solution:** I will explicitly model the difference between "parroting" (repeating) and "paraphrasing" (summarizing the core idea) during the input stage.<br><br>**Problem 2:** Dominant speakers may take over the group activity.<br>**Solution:** The activity rules will mandate that a student *must* paraphrase the previous speaker before they are allowed to introduce a new point. |
| **Anticipated Problems — Trainer / Materials** | **Problem:** The activity might end too quickly if learners run out of things to say on the topic.<br>**Solution:** I will prepare 3 backup scenario cards with controversial but safe topics (e.g., "Should college attendance be mandatory?") to keep the conversation flowing. |

## PART D — Stage-by-Stage Lesson Procedure

| Stage Name & Aim | Time | Trainer Does / Says | Learners Do | Interaction Pattern & Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **STAGE 1: Lead-In / Warmer**<br>*Aim: Hook learners and activate prior knowledge about listening.* | 5 mins | Runs a quick game of "Telephone" or asks: *"Think of a time you were talking to someone, and you knew they weren't listening. What gave it away?"* Elicits behaviors (looking at phone, nodding blankly, interrupting). | Share experiences briefly. Generate a list of "Bad Listening Behaviors" on the board. | **WC, T→S**<br>*Rationale: Creates immediate personal relevance. Identifies bad habits to set the stage for good habits.* |
| **STAGE 2: Presentation / Input**<br>*Aim: Introduce the Paraphrasing Framework.* | 10 mins | Introduces the concept of "Paraphrasing." Writes functional phrases on the board:<br>- *"So, what you're saying is..."*<br>- *"If I understand correctly, you feel..."*<br>Models a short dialogue with a strong student. Asks CCQ: *"Did I add my own opinion first, or did I summarize their point first?"* | Listen, take notes on the functional phrases. Answer the CCQ ("Summarize first"). | **T→S, S→T**<br>*Rationale: Provides the specific linguistic tools (functional phrases) needed to execute the soft skill.* |
| **STAGE 3: Controlled Practice**<br>*Aim: Guided practice of paraphrasing in pairs.* | 10 mins | Provides a list of 5 strong opinions (e.g., "Social media is ruining productivity"). Instructions: *"Student A reads the sentence. Student B uses a phrase from the board to paraphrase it. Then swap."* Monitors and listens for correct usage. | Work in pairs. Take turns reading a statement and paraphrasing the core meaning back to the partner without arguing the point. | **S→S, IND**<br>*Rationale: Removes the cognitive load of coming up with an original idea, allowing them to focus purely on the target skill (paraphrasing).* |
| **STAGE 4: Free Practice** *(Recommended for Demo)*<br>*Aim: Apply the skill in a realistic, less-controlled context.* | 15 mins | Divides class into groups of 3 (Speaker, Responder, Observer). Gives a Discussion Prompt (e.g., "Work from Home vs. Office"). Rule: *"Before you share your opinion, you MUST paraphrase what the last person said."* The Observer uses a checklist to track if they did it. | Engage in a 3-way debate. Must actively listen to the previous speaker in order to paraphrase them successfully before advancing their own argument. Observers take notes. | **GW (S→S→S)**<br>*Rationale: Mimics a real-world Group Discussion (GD) or workplace meeting. The Observer role ensures accountability and active listening.* |
| **STAGE 5: Feedback & Reflection**<br>*Aim: Consolidate learning and ensure real-world transfer.* | 5 mins | Asks Observers to share what went well. Asks the class: *"How did it feel to be paraphrased?"* (Elicits: "I felt heard/respected"). Concludes with an Exit Ticket: *"Write down one situation next week where you will use this skill."* | Observers share feedback. Class reflects on the emotional impact of active listening. Complete the Exit Ticket. | **WC, IND**<br>*Rationale: Ties the mechanical skill back to emotional intelligence and real-world employability (GDs, interviews, teamwork).* |
