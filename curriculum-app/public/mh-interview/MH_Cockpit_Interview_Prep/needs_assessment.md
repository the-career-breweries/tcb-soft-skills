# Initial Learner Needs Assessment

## Overview
Before delivering targeted soft skills training, it is essential to establish the baseline communication proficiency, confidence levels, and professional awareness of the university students. This initial diagnostic ensures that subsequent sessions are appropriately scaffolded and directly address the learners' actual gaps, rather than assumed deficits.

## 1. Assessment Method: "The Speed Networking Diagnostic"
To assess learners without inducing test anxiety, I will use a low-stakes, interactive **Speed Networking** activity. 

**Duration:** 10 minutes
**Format:** 
- Students form two concentric circles facing each other (or sit in pairs across tables). 
- They are given a simple professional prompt: *"Introduce yourself, state your degree, and share one professional skill you want to learn this year."*
- They have 60 seconds to converse before the outer circle rotates to the next partner. We will complete 3 rotations.

**Trainer's Role:** During the activity, I will actively monitor (walk around) and take discrete notes using a standardized diagnostic rubric. I am listening for:
- **Fluency & Vocabulary:** Are they searching for basic words (A1/A2), using adequate but informal language (B1/B2), or deploying varied, precise vocabulary (C1/C2)?
- **Body Language & Presence:** Are they making eye contact? How is their posture and vocal projection?
- **Interaction & Listening:** Are they asking follow-up questions or just waiting for their turn to speak?

## 2. Identifying Gaps Between Current Ability and Learning Goals
Following the activity, I will map the observational data against the target learning outcomes for the cohort. 

- **If the goal is "Interview Readiness" and the baseline is A1/A2:** The gap is foundational. They need vocabulary building, sentence frames, and basic confidence building before tackling complex interview questions.
- **If the goal is "Interview Readiness" and the baseline is B1/B2:** The gap is refinement. They have the fluency but lack professional structure (e.g., they ramble). 
- **If the goal is "Interview Readiness" and the baseline is C1/C2:** The gap is nuance. They need to learn how to pivot difficult questions, use persuasive storytelling, and project executive presence.

## 3. Adapting Lesson Plans Based on CEFR Levels
The results of this 10-minute diagnostic will directly dictate how I facilitate the curriculum:

### For an A1 / A2 Cohort (Beginner - Elementary)
- **Adaptation:** High structure, high scaffolding. 
- **Action:** I will provide clear sentence starters (e.g., *"Good morning, my name is..."*). Instructions will be short and staged. Visual aids and written instructions will accompany all verbal directions. I will minimize trainer talk-time to maximize their speaking practice in safe, paired environments.

### For a B1 / B2 Cohort (Intermediate)
- **Adaptation:** Focus on functional language and professionalization.
- **Action:** I will introduce frameworks (like STAR or PREP) to structure their existing fluency. I will increase the complexity of role-plays and introduce peer-feedback mechanics, shifting the focus from "getting the words out" to "saying it professionally."

### For a C1 / C2 Cohort (Advanced - Proficient)
- **Adaptation:** High autonomy, focus on nuance and critical thinking.
- **Action:** I will remove basic scaffolding and instead introduce complex, unpredictable scenarios (e.g., handling a hostile interview question, resolving a team conflict). The focus will shift heavily toward self-reflection, micro-expressions, tone, and strategic communication.
