# Round 2 Demo Strategy & Master Scripts

This document outlines how to execute the three 10-minute demos, what to say, and how to handle the panel interview. The goal is to show the panel you understand **differentiation**—how a trainer's energy, vocabulary, and facilitation style must change depending on the learners' CEFR level.

---

## 1. STRATEGY FOR THE THREE DEMOS

For a strict 10-minute demo, you cannot teach a whole lesson. You must drop the panel directly into a specific stage. **Treat the panel as your students.** 

### Demo 1: A1/A2 (Beginner)
- **Stage Chosen:** Stage 3 (Controlled Practice)
- **Trainer Persona:** Highly encouraging, loud, clear, slow pacing. Lots of smiling.
- **Differentiation:** You will use exaggerated body language, very simple vocabulary, and heavy scaffolding (whiteboard/handouts). You will demonstrate the activity yourself before asking them to do it.

### Demo 2: B1/B2 (Intermediate)
- **Stage Chosen:** Stage 4 (Free Practice - Setup & Execution)
- **Trainer Persona:** Professional, structured, facilitator. 
- **Differentiation:** You will speak at a normal conversational pace. You will focus heavily on clear instructions and Concept Checking Questions (CCQs) to ensure they understand the "rules" of the activity before they begin.

### Demo 3: C1/C2 (Advanced)
- **Stage Chosen:** Stage 2 (Presentation/Input - The PREP Framework)
- **Trainer Persona:** Executive coach, authoritative but consultative, peer-to-peer respect.
- **Differentiation:** You will use sophisticated vocabulary. You will challenge them intellectually. You will not over-explain; you will trust their fluency and focus purely on the structural framework.

## 2. DETAILED TRAINER SCRIPTS (10 Minutes Each)

### Demo 1 Script: A1/A2 (Self-Introduction)
*(Write the 3-sentence frame on a whiteboard before starting).*

**[0:00 - 0:02] The Hook (Energy: High & Warm)**
"Good morning, class! Today, we are learning how to introduce ourselves. Not to our friends, but to a boss. Let's look at me. *(Slouch, look at the floor, mumble)* 'Hi, I'm [Name].' ... Was that good? No! Why? *(Elicit from panel: "No eye contact, too quiet").* Exactly! Now watch this. *(Stand straight, smile, make eye contact).* 'Good morning. My name is [Name].' Better? Yes! Eye contact is power."

**[0:02 - 0:05] The Setup (Scaffolding)**
"Look at the board. We have three sentences. 
1. 'Good morning, my name is...' 
2. 'I am studying...' 
3. 'I am happy to meet you.' 
Let's practice together. Repeat after me! *(Have the panel repeat each sentence chorally).* Excellent."

**[0:05 - 0:09] The Practice (Facilitation)**
"Now, you will do it. I have a paper for you. *(Hand out a prop/paper).* Write your name. Write your degree. 
Now, stand up! *(Motion for the panel to stand).* Face a partner. You are going to say your three sentences. But remember our rule: What must you do with your eyes? *(Elicit: "Look at them").* Yes! Make eye contact. Go!"
*(Walk around, smile, observe. Do not interrupt them while they speak).*

**[0:09 - 0:10] Assessment & Close**
"Stop! Please sit down. Who had great eye contact? *(Let them point to a peer).* Excellent job. Tomorrow, we will practice handshakes. Thank you!"

### Demo 2 Script: B1/B2 (Paraphrasing)

**[0:00 - 0:03] The Context (Energy: Structured & Engaging)**
"Welcome back, everyone. In group discussions, we all know how to speak. But do we know how to listen? Often, we just wait for our turn to talk. Today, we are practicing 'Paraphrasing'—summarizing what someone else said before you share your own opinion. This shows respect and emotional intelligence."

**[0:03 - 0:06] The Framework & CCQs**
"On the board, I have two phrases: 'So, what you're saying is...' and 'If I understand correctly...' 
Let's test this. *(Point to a panelist).* Tell me your favorite movie and why. *(Listen).* 
Okay, class, watch me: 'So, what you're saying is you love Interstellar because of the soundtrack. I agree, Hans Zimmer is amazing.' 
*Concept Check Question:* Class, did I share my opinion first, or did I summarize their point first? *(Elicit: Summarize first).* Exactly."

**[0:06 - 0:09] The Activity Setup**
"We are going to do a rapid-fire debate. I will give you a topic: 'Is work-from-home better than working in an office?' 
The rule is: You cannot share your opinion until you paraphrase the person who spoke before you using a phrase from the board. 
*ICQ (Instruction Check Question):* Can you just state your opinion immediately? *(Elicit: No).* What must you do first? *(Elicit: Paraphrase).* Perfect. Begin!"
*(Let the panel argue for 2 minutes. Step back. Take notes on a clipboard).*

**[0:09 - 0:10] Reflection**
"Time! How did it feel when the person before you actually summarized your point? *(Elicit answers).* It builds instant rapport. Great job today."

### Demo 3 Script: C1/C2 (PREP Framework)

**[0:00 - 0:03] The Provocation (Energy: Executive, calm, authoritative)**
"Good afternoon. You all have excellent fluency. You speak English beautifully. But in an executive interview, fluency can actually be your enemy. When we are nervous, and we are fluent, we ramble. We over-explain. We lose the interviewer's attention. Today, we fix that. We are learning the PREP framework for executive conciseness."

**[0:03 - 0:07] The Demonstration**
"PREP stands for Point, Reason, Example, Point. Let's take a difficult interview question: *'Why should we hire you over other candidates?'* 
Without PREP, I might tell a 3-minute story about my childhood. With PREP, it sounds like this:
*(Shift posture, deliver sharply)*: 
'You should hire me because of my adaptability **(Point)**. In this industry, software changes every six months **(Reason)**. For example, during my last internship, our client changed the entire project scope two weeks before the deadline. I taught myself the new software over the weekend and delivered the project on time **(Example)**. That ability to adapt under pressure is why I will add immediate value to your team **(Point)**.'
That was 35 seconds. It was bulletproof."

**[0:07 - 0:10] The Challenge**
"Now, it's your turn in the hot seat. I have prompt cards here with difficult questions. You have exactly 60 seconds to prepare a PREP outline in your notebook, and then you will deliver it to the person next to you. Your partner will time you. If you go over 60 seconds, they will cut you off. Ready? Your question is: *'Tell me about a time you failed.'* Go."

## 3. ANTICIPATED PANEL QUESTIONS & STRONG ANSWERS

**Q: Why didn't you correct the student when they made a grammar mistake during the role-play?**
*A: "During 'Free Practice' stages, the focus is on fluency and confidence, not accuracy. If I interrupt them to fix grammar, I destroy their confidence and the flow of the activity. I take notes of systemic errors and address them anonymously during the feedback stage at the end."*

**Q: How do you know they actually learned anything in that 10 minutes?**
*A: "I used Concept Checking Questions (CCQs). Instead of asking 'Do you understand?', which usually just gets a polite nod, I asked specific questions like 'Did I share my opinion first or summarize first?' Their correct answer proved they understood the mechanics before the activity started."*

**Q: What would you do if a student in the B1/B2 class completely dominated the discussion?**
*A: "I would use the structure of the activity to manage them. For example, by introducing a 'talking token' (like a pen), or explicitly assigning them the role of the 'Observer' for one round. This channels their energy into listening without making them feel punished."*

## 4. POST-DEMO REFLECTION STRATEGY (Part E)

Within 24 hours of your interview, you must email your reflection. Use this structure:
1. **What went well:** Mention a specific moment where the panel (acting as students) successfully executed the skill based on your instructions. *"The transition into the role-play worked well because the CCQs ensured everyone knew the rules."*
2. **What I would change:** Show self-awareness. *"If I had a full hour, I would have spent more time eliciting examples from the learners during the input stage, rather than providing the models myself, to increase student talk-time (STT)."*
3. **Evidence of learning:** Point to observable behavior. *"I knew learning occurred when [Panelist Name] successfully used the phrase 'So what you're saying is...' without looking at the whiteboard."*
