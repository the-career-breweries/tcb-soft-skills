## PART B — Context, Assumptions & Anticipated Problems

| Field | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Learner Profile & Assumed Knowledge** | Advanced university students (e.g., final year PG) who are highly fluent in English. They have strong vocabularies and can converse easily on complex topics. However, under pressure, they may waffle, over-explain, or lose their core message. They need frameworks for executive conciseness. |
| **Personal Aim (Trainer Development)** | Because this group is highly fluent, my aim is to step back entirely during the free practice stage. I want to facilitate peer-to-peer feedback rather than providing all the feedback myself, acting as a guide rather than a lecturer. |
| **Anticipated Problems — Learners** | **Problem 1:** Learners may resist using a rigid framework, feeling it restricts their natural fluency.<br>**Solution:** I will frame PREP and STAR not as "scripts," but as "anchors" that prevent them from rambling when nervous.<br><br>**Problem 2:** Learners may provide STAR examples that are too long (too much Situation, not enough Action).<br>**Solution:** Set strict time limits (e.g., 60 seconds total for a response) to force conciseness. |
| **Anticipated Problems — Trainer / Materials** | **Problem:** The "difficult questions" might be too easy for this cohort.<br>**Solution:** I will prepare highly behavioral, nuanced questions (e.g., "Tell me about a time you fundamentally disagreed with a professor's feedback") rather than generic ones. |

## PART C — Materials & Resources

**Materials List:**
1. Whiteboard & Markers (Trainer).
2. "Hostile / Difficult Question" prompt cards (Trainer prepares).
3. Stopwatch or timer visible to the room.

## PART D — Stage-by-Stage Lesson Procedure

| Stage Name & Aim | Time | Trainer Does / Says | Learners Do | Interaction Pattern & Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **STAGE 1: Lead-In / Warmer**<br>*Aim: Engage learners with the concept of pressure and conciseness.* | 5 mins | Projects or writes an interview question: *"Why should we hire you?"* Asks one confident student to answer it on the spot. Usually, they ramble. Asks: *"What happens to our communication when we are put on the spot?"* | Volunteer attempts to answer. Class discusses the tendency to over-explain or lose the main point when nervous. | **WC, T→S, S→T**<br>*Rationale: Highlights the gap in their current skillset. Fluency is not enough; structure is needed.* |
| **STAGE 2: Input (PREP)**<br>*Aim: Introduce the PREP Framework for opinion/persuasive questions.* | 10 mins | Introduces PREP: **P**oint, **R**eason, **E**xample, **P**oint. Models answering *"Why should we hire you?"* in under 45 seconds using PREP. Asks CCQs: *"Did I start with a story or a direct statement?"* | Listen and analyze the trainer's model against the framework. Notice the difference in impact. | **T→S**<br>*Rationale: Advanced learners need to see a sophisticated demonstration to buy into the framework.* |
| **STAGE 3: Input (STAR)**<br>*Aim: Introduce the STAR Framework for behavioral/descriptive questions.* | 10 mins | Transitions to descriptive questions: *"What about when asked to describe a strength, weakness, or past event?"* Introduces STAR: **S**ituation, **T**ask, **A**ction, **R**esult. Models a concise STAR response. | Listen and differentiate between when to use PREP (opinions/persuasion) vs STAR (stories/behavior). | **T→S**<br>*Rationale: Equips learners with the second essential toolkit for descriptive scenarios.* |
| **STAGE 4: Controlled Practice**<br>*Aim: Guided application of the frameworks.* | 10 mins | Hands out two scenarios (one persuasive, one behavioral). Instructions: *"Take 3 minutes to write bullet points for a PREP answer and a STAR answer."* Then pairs them up to deliver to each other. | Work individually to outline points. Deliver the structured answers to a partner. | **IND, S→S**<br>*Rationale: Gives them processing time to map complex thoughts into the structures before spontaneous performance.* |
| **STAGE 5: Free Practice / The 'Hot Seat'**<br>*Aim: Test the skill under pressure with impromptu questions.* | 20 mins | Sets up groups of 3 (Interviewer, Candidate, Evaluator). The Interviewer draws a "Difficult Question" card. Candidate must identify if it needs PREP or STAR, and answer immediately. Evaluator runs a 60-second timer. | Rotate roles so everyone is in the 'Hot Seat'. Candidates must think on their feet and deliver structured answers under 60 seconds. | **GW (S→S→S)**<br>*Rationale: Simulates real-world executive pressure. Peer evaluation forces them to internalize the frameworks.* |
| **STAGE 6: Reflection & Closure**<br>*Aim: Consolidate learning and peer feedback.* | 5 mins | Facilitates a group debrief. Asks Evaluators: *"What was the hardest part for the Candidates?"* Asks learners to reflect on how PREP and STAR can be used outside of interviews. | Share insights from the hot-seat activity. Reflect on broader applications for the frameworks. | **WC, S→T**<br>*Rationale: C1/C2 learners benefit immensely from metacognition and analyzing communication mechanics.* |
