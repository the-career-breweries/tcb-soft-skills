## PART B — Context, Assumptions & Anticipated Problems

| Field | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Learner Profile & Assumed Knowledge** | First/second-year university students from regional language backgrounds or with limited exposure to corporate English. They understand basic English but lack speaking confidence and professional vocabulary. They are familiar with casual greetings (e.g., "What's up") but may struggle with formal variants. |
| **Personal Aim (Trainer Development)** | I want to ensure my instructions are graded (simplified) and avoid using complex idioms. I want to improve my use of visual modeling before asking students to perform. |
| **Anticipated Problems — Learners** | **Problem 1:** Learners may feel too shy to speak up in front of the group.<br>**Solution:** I will use pair work (S→S) first, so they practice in a low-stakes environment before speaking to the whole class.<br><br>**Problem 2:** Learners may memorize the sentence frame but deliver it looking at the floor.<br>**Solution:** I will explicitly model the difference between "looking down" and "making eye contact," and make eye contact a specific checklist item for peer feedback. |
| **Anticipated Problems — Trainer / Materials** | **Problem:** The projector fails, meaning they can't see the sentence frames.<br>**Solution:** I will write the sentence frame on the whiteboard in clear, large handwriting and have printed backup handouts. |

## PART C — Materials & Resources

**Materials List:**
1. Whiteboard & Markers (Trainer) - To write the 3-sentence frame.
2. Printed "Introduction Framework" handouts with fill-in-the-blanks (Trainer prepares, 1 per student).
3. "Peer Feedback" checklist cards (Smile = Good Eye Contact, Straight Face = Needs Work) (Trainer prepares).

## PART D — Stage-by-Stage Lesson Procedure

| Stage Name & Aim | Time | Trainer Does / Says | Learners Do | Interaction Pattern & Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **STAGE 1: Lead-In / Warmer**<br>*Aim: Engage learners and set the context for first impressions.* | 5 mins | Shows two pictures: Person A slouching and looking down, Person B smiling with good posture. Asks: *"Who would you hire for a job? Why?"* Elicits the word "confident." | Look at pictures, point, and call out simple answers ("Person B", "Happy", "Confident"). | **T→S, WC**<br>*Rationale: Creates immediate visual relevance without requiring complex vocabulary.* |
| **STAGE 2: Presentation / Input**<br>*Aim: Introduce the target language (formal greetings & 3-sentence frame).* | 10 mins | Writes on board:<br>1. *"Good morning, my name is..."*<br>2. *"I am studying..."*<br>3. *"I am happy to meet you."*<br>Models the introduction twice: once poorly (looking down, mumbling) and once well. Asks CCQ: *"Did I look at you the first time or the second time?"* | Watch the demonstration. Answer the CCQ ("The second time"). Copy the frame into their notebooks or fill in the handout. | **T→S**<br>*Rationale: Provides highly structured, visual input. The contrast model clearly demonstrates the soft skill (body language).* |
| **STAGE 3: Controlled Practice** *(Recommended for Demo)*<br>*Aim: Practise the skill in a supported environment.* | 10 mins | Distributes fill-in-the-blank handouts. Gives instruction: *"Fill in your name and degree. Read it silently."* Then: *"Stand up. Find a partner. Say your introduction. Look at their eyes."* Monitors actively but does not interrupt. | Fill in their details. Stand up, pair up, and read their introduction to their partner. Focus on getting the words right and maintaining eye contact. | **IND, S→S**<br>*Rationale: Allows safe practice. Standing up changes the physical energy of the room and builds physical confidence.* |
| **STAGE 4: Free Practice**<br>*Aim: Apply the skill in a slightly less controlled context.* | 15 mins | Sets up a "Networking Party." Instruction: *"Walk around the room. You must introduce yourself to 3 new people. Shake hands if comfortable. Smile."* Plays soft background music to lower anxiety. | Walk around, meet multiple peers, and deliver the introduction from memory rather than reading the paper. | **S→S, WC**<br>*Rationale: Moves from reading a script to authentic interaction, building fluency and muscle memory.* |
| **STAGE 5: Feedback & Correction**<br>*Aim: Address errors and consolidate learning.* | 5 mins | Brings class back to seats. Asks: *"Who had great eye contact?"* Gives positive feedback: *"I heard excellent clear voices."* Corrects one common error gently (e.g., pronunciation of a specific word) without naming individuals. | Nominate peers who did well. Listen to feedback. Repeat the corrected pronunciation together. | **T→S, WC**<br>*Rationale: Celebrates success, builds morale, and corrects systemic errors safely.* |
