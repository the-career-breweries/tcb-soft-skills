# The 5 Families of Irregular Verbs: A Quick Reference Guide

English irregular verbs don't follow the modern "-ed" rule. Instead, they are ancient survivors that fall into **five main pattern families**. 

Use this cheat sheet to quickly identify which family a verb belongs to!

---

### Family 1: The "All Identical" Family (V1 = V2 = V3)
These verbs are stubborn—they never change their shape, no matter what time it is.
*   **Put** / Put / Put
*   **Cut** / Cut / Cut
*   **Hit** / Hit / Hit
*   **Cost** / Cost / Cost
*   **Set** / Set / Set
> *Aviation Context:* "The ground crew **set** the chocks yesterday, and they have **set** them again today."

---

### Family 2: The "Twins" Family (V2 = V3)
For these verbs, the Simple Past (V2) and the Past Participle (V3) are identical. 
*   **Build** / Built / Built
*   **Leave** / Left / Left
*   **Keep** / Kept / Kept
*   **Find** / Found / Found
*   **Catch** / Caught / Caught
*   **Buy** / Bought / Bought
> *Aviation Context:* "The flight **left** on time. In fact, it has **left** on time all week."

---

### Family 3: The "Boomerang" Family (V1 = V3)
These verbs "boomerang" right back to their original base form for the V3 slot. 
*   **Come** / Came / **Come**
*   **Run** / Ran / **Run**
*   **Become** / Became / **Become**
> *Aviation Context:* "The captain **came** aboard. He had already **come** aboard before the passengers."

---

### Family 4: The "Vowel Shift (i-a-u)" Family
These verbs change their inner vowel from **i** (present) to **a** (past) to **u** (helper past).
*   **Begin** / Began / Begun
*   **Ring** / Rang / Rung
*   **Drink** / Drank / Drunk
*   **Sing** / Sang / Sung
*   **Sink** / Sank / Sunk
> *Aviation Context:* "The boarding process **began** at 10:00. It had **begun** before I arrived."

---

### Family 5: The "-en Ending" Family
These verbs use a variety of V2 forms, but they almost always end in **"-en"** for their V3 form.
*   **Speak** / Spoke / Spok**en**
*   **Take** / Took / Tak**en**
*   **Break** / Broke / Brok**en**
*   **Fly** / Flew / Flown *(ends in an 'n' sound)*
*   **Drive** / Drove / Driv**en**
*   **Write** / Wrote / Writt**en**
*   **Fall** / Fell / Fall**en**
> *Aviation Context:* "The pilot **flew** the aircraft. She has **flown** this route many times."
