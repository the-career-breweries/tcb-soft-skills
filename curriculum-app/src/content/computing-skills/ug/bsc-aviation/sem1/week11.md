# 🧠 Key Concept

- Slide Layouts & The 6×6 Rule Explained

- Common slide layouts:


### 🔹 The 6×6 Rule
- A slide should have at most 6 bullet points, and each bullet should have at most 6 words. This keeps a slide readable at a glance and stops it turning into a script read word-for-word to the audience.
- Fonts should be large enough to read from the back of the room: at least 24pt for body text and 36pt for titles.

---

# 🧠 Key Concept

- Transitions & Presenting with Confidence

- A transition is a short animation that plays as one slide changes to the next — for example, a fade or a wipe. Transitions should be simple and consistent, since flashy effects distract from the message rather than support it.

- Two ways to start a slide show:

- Presenting with confidence: make eye contact, speak slowly, and use slides to support what is said — not to read from directly.

- Quick Tips
- **Less text per slide** — you explain the rest when presenting
- Use large readable font (24 pt+ for body, 36 pt+ for titles)
- F5 starts slideshow from beginning; Shift+F5 from current slide
- 6×6 rule: max 6 bullets, max 6 words per bullet
- **One main idea per slide** — don't overcrowd
