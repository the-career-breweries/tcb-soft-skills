# Fundamentals of Grammar
Welcome to Week 1. Today we cover Parts of Speech, Tenses, and Sentence Structure.

---

# The Noun Cluster
These four parts of speech are all deeply connected to **things, people, and concepts**.

*   **Noun:** The core "thing" itself. *(e.g., The **aircraft**)*
*   **Pronoun:** The substitute for the noun. *(e.g., **It** is ready)*
*   **Adjective:** The descriptor of the noun. *(e.g., The **heavy** aircraft)*
*   **Preposition:** Shows the physical/time relationship of the noun to something else. *(e.g., **Inside** the aircraft, **before** the flight)*

---

# Actions & Enhancers (Verbs & Adverbs)
This cluster drives the momentum of the sentence.

*   **Verb:** The action or state of being. *(e.g., The pilot **navigated** through the storm.)*
*   **Adverb:** Modifies the verb, an adjective, or another adverb. *(e.g., The pilot navigated **skillfully**.)* 
    * *Note:* In rare, advanced cases, adverbs can even modify a noun phrase! *(e.g., **Even** the captain was surprised. "**Only** aviation students understand this.)*

---

# The Connectors: Conjunctions
Conjunctions act as the glue that binds clauses, phrases, and words together so we don't speak in choppy, robotic sentences.

*   **Coordinating (FANBOYS):** For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So.
    *(e.g., We requested clearance, **but** the runway was occupied.)*
*   **Subordinating:** Because, Although, Since.
    *(e.g., **Although** it was raining, the flight departed on time.)*

---

# The Emotive Words: Interjections
Interjections are words or phrases that stand entirely on their own to express sudden emotion, surprise, or urgency. They are grammatically disconnected from the rest of the sentence.

*   *(e.g., **Brace!** The impact is imminent.)*
*   *(e.g., **Oh**, the flight is delayed again?)*
*   *(e.g., **Wow**, that landing was incredibly smooth.)*

---

# Sentence Structure: The Golden Rule
The most important rule in English sentence structure is **Subject-Verb Agreement**. 

The subject (the "who" or "what") must always match the verb in number.
*   **Singular Subject -> Singular Verb** (usually ends in 's')
    *(e.g., The pilot **checks** the instruments.)*
*   **Plural Subject -> Plural Verb** (no 's')
    *(e.g., The pilots **check** the instruments.)*

---

# Subject-Verb Agreement: Tricky Cases
Sometimes, it's hard to tell if the subject is singular or plural.

*   **Group Nouns (usually singular):** The *crew* **is** ready. The *team* **has** boarded.
*   **"And" (plural):** The captain *and* the first officer **are** in the cockpit.
*   **"Or" (matches closest noun):** The flight attendants or the *captain* **makes** the announcement.

# Activity: Sentence Structure
Identify the Subject and Verb, and check if they agree:

> "The fleet of new aircraft **are** arriving tomorrow."
*   *Wait!* What is the true subject? 
*   "Fleet" (singular group). 
*   *Correction:* "The fleet of new aircraft **is** arriving tomorrow."

# Tenses Overview
*   **Past:** Actions completed before now (e.g., *The flight landed.*)
*   **Present:** Actions happening now or habitually (e.g., *The flight lands at 5 PM.*)
*   **Future:** Actions that will happen (e.g., *The flight will land.*)
